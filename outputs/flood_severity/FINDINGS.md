# Flood damage vs. severity: findings (1999-2025)

Generated by `scripts/flood_damage_severity.py`. Event types: Flood + Flash Flood, 50 states + DC.
Money is property damage in Aug 2026 dollars (CPI-U). Every number is recomputed on each run; do not edit by hand.

**160,094 flood events** in **51,765 episodes**, $159.1B property damage, 2,366 direct deaths.

## Headline

- **Footprint is the strongest severity signal.** A flood episode that hits 16+ counties averages $71.9M in damage, vs $654K for a one-county flood (~110x). Damage grows faster than footprint: elasticity 0.99 (95% CI 0.95-1.02), so doubling the counties hit multiplies damage by about 2.0x.
- **Wide floods are also far deadlier:** 693 deaths per 1,000 episodes at 16+ counties vs 14 for one county. 16+ county episodes are 2.6% of episodes but 60% of damage and 40% of deaths.
- **Extent and depth point the same way.** Floods spanning 10+ miles average $6.3M vs $151K under half a mile. Where the narrative quotes a depth, 8+ ft floods average $8.4M vs $461K under 1 ft.
- **Cause matters:** floods from tropical systems average $22.2M per event; they are 2.3% of events and 43% of damage.
- **Extremely concentrated:** the costliest 1% of events (with a damage estimate) carry 92% of all flood damage; the costliest 0.1% carry 72%.
- **Flash floods kill more:** 1,762 deaths (17.9 per 1k events) vs 604 for river/areal floods (9.8 per 1k).

## Caveats

- **Years before 2007 are under-recorded.** 62% of 1999-2006 flood events have a blank damage field, vs 0% from 2007 on. Blank = no estimate: those events are kept for counts, deaths and flood size, add $0 to totals, and are left out of per-event damage averages and shares,
  and 30% of 1999-2006 events were recorded by forecast zone rather than county (no county FIPS). Treat 1999-2006 damage totals as a lower bound.
- **No direct flood magnitude.** Storm Events has no flood gauge height or rainfall field; all six measures are proxies.
- **Footprint and extent are partly scale, not intensity.** A wide flood can be shallow. They measure how much area is exposed.
- **Depth covers few events** and comes from free-text narratives parsed with a regex. An LLM could extract depth (and rainfall, rescues, homes flooded) from many more narratives; this is a natural AI extension.
- **Damage is a NWS estimate, not insured loss.** Many events carry $0 because no estimate was made; Helene (2024) is far below published totals.
- **Averages are driven by a few huge events** (e.g. Harvey 2017). Medians of damaged events are shown next to them.
- **Deaths use DEATHS_DIRECT only.**
