# Flood damage vs. severity: findings (2007-2025)

Generated by `scripts/flood_damage_severity.py`. Event types: Flood + Flash Flood, 50 states + DC.
Money is property damage in 2025 dollars (CPI-U adjusted). Every number is recomputed on each run; do not edit by hand.

**120,768 flood events** in **32,095 episodes**, $130.8B property damage, 1,891 direct deaths.

## Headline

- **Footprint is the strongest severity signal.** A flood episode that hits 16+ counties averages $75.0M in damage, vs $154K for a one-county flood (~490x). Damage grows faster than footprint: elasticity 1.15 (95% CI 1.11-1.19), so doubling the counties hit multiplies damage by about 2.2x.
- **Wide floods are also far deadlier:** 749 deaths per 1,000 episodes at 16+ counties vs 14 for one county. 16+ county episodes are 3.7% of episodes but 69% of damage and 47% of deaths.
- **Extent and depth point the same way.** Floods spanning 10+ miles average $6.1M vs $85K under half a mile. Where the narrative quotes a depth, 8+ ft floods average $6.7M vs $435K under 1 ft.
- **Cause matters:** floods from tropical systems average $21.4M per event; they are 2.6% of events and 51% of damage.
- **Extremely concentrated:** the costliest 1% of events carry 95% of all flood damage; the costliest 0.1% carry 78%.
- **Flash floods kill more:** 1,404 deaths (19.1 per 1k events) vs 487 for river/areal floods (10.3 per 1k).

Elasticity = % change in damage for a 1% change in the proxy. R² values are modest: each proxy explains part of the variation, and damage also depends on what the water hits (homes, roads, farmland).

## Caveats

- **No direct flood magnitude.** Storm Events has no flood gauge height or rainfall field; all six measures are proxies.
- **Footprint and extent are partly scale, not intensity.** A wide flood can be shallow. They measure how much area is exposed.
- **Depth covers few events** and comes from free-text narratives parsed with a regex. An LLM could extract depth (and rainfall, rescues, homes flooded) from many more narratives; this is a natural AI extension.
- **Damage is a NWS estimate, not insured loss.** Many events carry $0 because no estimate was made; Helene (2024) is far below published totals.
- **Averages are driven by a few huge events** (e.g. Harvey 2017). Medians of damaged events are shown next to them.
- **Deaths use DEATHS_DIRECT only.**
